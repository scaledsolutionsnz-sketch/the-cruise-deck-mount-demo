The problem this site solves

Everyone who writes about it uses the same word.

Independent sources describing the Cruise Deck converge on one idea: it is hidden. One former tenant was literally trading under the name Hideaway. That is charming in a review and expensive in a lease.

  • "hidden gem"Recurring in public descriptions of the complex
  • "tucked away" · "at the back" · "off the main street"How visitors describe finding it

A directory with a map is a literal fix for a literal problem. The arcade is invisible from the footpath of the Mount's main shopping street. Foot traffic passes the entrance rather than entering it.

You cannot move the building. You can make sure that when somebody searches for a florist, a gluten free lunch, a laundromat or a tattoo studio in Mount Maunganui, the arcade appears, with a plan showing exactly where each door is.

Getting here

From the cruise berth, on foot.

On a ship day the arcade is a short flat walk from the gangway. It is worth saying out loud, because a passenger with four hours ashore will not gamble on an unmarked laneway.

The same map serves a local looking for the florist, a family looking for a covered courtyard out of the wind, and somebody searching gluten free Mount Maunganui in the middle of July. Cruise is the second sentence on this site, not the first.

The case that survives it

This is a capture rate argument, not a growth argument.

Ships still range from the 184 passengers of the Heritage Adventurer up to the 4,905 of Anthem of the Seas. On a big day, several thousand people walk into a township of that size within a five to ten minute radius of the arcade.

The site does not claim to bring more ships. It changes the odds that somebody already standing on Maunganui Road turns into the entry instead of walking past it. If a directory converts even a small extra share of an existing crowd, it pays for itself out of the same crowd that arrived anyway.

And it keeps working in June, when there are no ships at all.

What this concept is, and is not

Everything above is real except the pictures and the hours.

Tenants, shop numbers, the vacancy, the cruise figures and the description of the site are drawn from confirmed sources. Two things are deliberately marked as placeholder, because inventing them would be worse than leaving them open.

  • Photography of the arcade is still needed. There are no usable photos of the complex in circulation. Facebook and TripAdvisor both blocked access during research. Every image on this page is licensed Mount Maunganui stock, used as atmosphere only, and none of them show the Cruise Deck. A half day shoot covering the entry, the courtyard, the signage and each shopfront would replace all of them.
  • Trading hours are placeholders. The open and closed logic is real and working. The hours behind it are written for demonstration. Each tenant confirms their own, once, and then the arcade owns the canonical version.
  • The tenant list should be signed off by Guideline. Several businesses that appear in third party directories for this address could not be confirmed as current and have been deliberately left off rather than guessed at. That omission is itself the argument for a single controlled source.
  • The plan is indicative. Unit positions show the relationship between street, entry and tenancy, not a survey. A walk through fixes it in an hour.

The proposal, for Guideline Investors

Not a marketing spend. A line in the building outgoings.

Tenants already fund roughly $122 per square metre per year of operating expenses across this building: the things that keep a complex functioning. A directory that tells people the complex exists belongs in that category, not in a discretionary marketing budget that nobody has approved.

01

Search visibility thirteen small businesses cannot buy alone

Several tenants here have no website at all. Individually they are invisible online. Aggregated under one address, with one set of structured hours, categories and a map, they rank as a destination. A florist, a laundromat and a gluten free lunch spot pull completely different searches into the same doorway.

02

One canonical source for hours and contact details

Third party directories already describe this complex inaccurately, listing businesses that are no longer here and hours that are not current. Guideline has no control over any of them and no way to correct them at the source. An owned site becomes the thing those directories copy from, and the thing Google trusts.

03

The vacancy tile is a leasing tool

Shop 2B is advertised at $23,900 plus GST plus OPEX. On this site it is a permanent, indexed, always current listing sitting inside a page that prospective tenants and their agents will already be reading to size up the neighbours. For a syndicator, that turns a marketing site into a leasing asset, and it is the strongest commercial argument on this page.

04

A template, not a one off

Guideline manages 23 properties, more than 60 tenants and around 260 investors from a dated site that distributes PDF flyers. The Cruise Deck is the hardest version of the problem: multi tenant, hospitality heavy, physically hidden. Solve it here and the same directory, map and vacancy pattern drops onto the other 22 properties at a fraction of the cost of the first one.

How to think about the number

The right comparison is not what a marketing agency charges for a website. It is what this building already spends per square metre, every year, on being a functioning place of business. Against $122 per square metre per year of existing outgoings, a directory that makes the complex findable is a small, recurring, allocable line item, and one that a leasing outcome on Shop 2B alone would cover.

Scope, hosting and the maintenance model are for a conversation. What is on this page is the product, not the invoice.

One thing to confirm before anything else The property is owned by a syndicate, Boulevard Investment Group, and managed by Guideline Investors Limited. We would want to know whether sign off on something like this sits with Guideline as manager, out of building outgoings, or whether it needs to go to the investor group. That answer changes the shape of the proposal and the timeline, so it is worth settling first.
